Project Overview

Partydeco specializes in wedding ring boxes and jewelry boxes. In Indonesia, wedding jewelry (mahar) is one of the most meaningful parts of the ceremony — valuable, and the literal centerpiece during the akad. I created a TikTok video encouraging couples to make their mahar stand out on their special day, developed end to end: hook, concept, filming, editing, on-screen copy, publishing, and hashtags.

Goal

Generate sales by increasing purchase intent for Partydeco's wedding ring box.

Target Audience

Couples aged 22–39 preparing for their wedding within the next few months.

Consumer Insight

Couples already know their wedding jewelry will be one of the most meaningful and valuable items during the ceremony — but many don't realize presentation matters just as much. If the jewelry is the centerpiece of the akad, it deserves to be presented beautifully. That became the hook: "Biar maharmu cakep di meja akad." ("Make your wedding jewelry look beautiful on the akad table.")

Content Strategy
  1. Started with an emotional hook — to stop the right audience from scrolling and make them curious enough to keep watching.
  2. Showed the product's beauty first — since customers buy largely on appearance, the opening shot led with the most beautiful angle before any features.
  3. Built trust for online buyers — Partydeco has one physical gallery but serves 35+ cities across Indonesia, so I used close-up, multi-angle shots of craftsmanship to reduce hesitation from buying sight-unseen.
  4. Reduced purchase anxiety — closed by clearly stating Partydeco's damage/loss replacement guarantee, addressing the biggest fear in online shopping: delivery.

Project Overview

This campaign designed to introduce the brand to couples looking for wedding accessory inspiration, rather than push directly for a sale. Developed end to end: content idea, hook, video concept, filming, editing, on-screen copy, publishing, hashtags, and performance analysis.

Goal

Build brand awareness by introducing Partydeco as a wedding accessory brand to couples preparing for their wedding.

Target Audience

Couples aged 22–39 preparing for their wedding within the next few months and looking for wedding inspiration, including accessories and ceremony details.

Consumer Insight

In Indonesia, many couples choose to marry on "beautiful dates" (tanggal cantik) — repeating numbers or memorable calendar dates — because they want their wedding day to feel more meaningful. I used this cultural behavior to create content that drew engagement from couples who already had a date and those still deciding. The resulting comments also became a live signal of which months had the highest wedding demand — intel Partydeco could use to time future marketing.

Content Strategy

Instead of promoting the ring box directly, I built content around a topic that naturally attracts the audience: popular wedding dates.

  1. Captured attention through a culturally relevant topic — a list of upcoming "beautiful wedding dates" as the hook.
  2. Encouraged interaction by prompting viewers to comment about their own wedding plans.
  3. Introduced the product naturally — Partydeco's ring box stayed in frame as the visual backdrop throughout, gaining repeated exposure without feeling like an ad.
  4. Increased brand familiarity among couples preparing for marriage through visual storytelling rather than a pitch.

Codename: BevZ Rollout MBA 6520, Predictive Analytics · Team Project
Predictive Model to Choose a Retail Rollout Strategy
Team of 3 · Built the prediction model comparing two go-to-market plans

Situation

BevZ, an energy-drink brand, needed to choose between two store-level rollout plans for its next scaling phase: a sales-rep-and-lower-price plan versus an endcap-and-demo marketing plan.

Action

Built a prediction model from historical test data to estimate how sales reps, endcaps, demos, and price each moved weekly unit sales, then applied it to both rollout plans to project weekly revenue, cost, and net revenue per store.

Result

The model showed the Marketing Plan generated significantly stronger unit lift from endcaps and demos than the sales-rep-led plan did from staffing, despite a higher retail price — and recommended the Marketing Plan to leadership.

Why This Matters Beyond Beverage Retail

The underlying skill here isn't specific to energy drinks — it's a repeatable way to de-risk any rollout decision. Any business scaling across multiple stores, regions, markets, or channels (retail, franchise, subscription, SaaS regional expansion) faces the same question: which investment actually moves the number, and by how much? Building a prediction model from small-scale test data — rather than rolling out on instinct or copying a competitor — lets a company quantify the return on each option before committing the full budget, and catch a costly mistake while it's still cheap to fix.
